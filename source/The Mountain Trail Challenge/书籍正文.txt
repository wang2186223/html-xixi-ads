### chapter 1
The Mountain Trail Challenge

Jordan Reed was afraid of heights. Not just a little afraid. Really, truly afraid. Climbing a ladder made his knees shake. Looking down from a tall building made his stomach twist. So when Coach Harper announced the eighth-grade end-of-year trip, Jordan wanted to hide under his desk.

"This year," Coach Harper said with a big smile, "we're going to climb Eagle Peak. It's a six-mile hike to the top, with the best view in the whole state. You'll work in teams of four, and every team must reach the summit together. No one gets left behind."

The class cheered. Jordan felt sick.

His team was made up of three people he barely knew. There was Aisha, the fastest runner in school, who was always first at everything. There was Tyler, a quiet boy who loved birds and carried binoculars everywhere. And there was Grace, who had recently broken her ankle playing basketball and had just finished physical therapy.

"Great," Aisha muttered when she saw the list. "We're going to be the slowest team on the mountain."

On the morning of the hike, the sky was clear and the air smelled of pine trees. The buses dropped the students at the bottom of the trail. Jordan looked up at the mountain. Eagle Peak rose high above the forest, its rocky top gleaming in the sun. His stomach flipped.

The first two miles were easy. The trail wound gently through tall trees, crossing small wooden bridges over sparkling streams. Tyler stopped every few minutes to point out birds.

"That's a red-tailed hawk," he said, pointing at a large bird circling overhead. "And listen, hear that tapping? A woodpecker."

Aisha rolled her eyes and walked ahead. "If we stop every five minutes, we'll never get to the top."

Grace was walking slowly, carefully placing each step. Jordan noticed that she winced sometimes when the trail went uphill.

"Are you okay?" he asked quietly.

"I'm fine," Grace said quickly. But she didn't look fine.

At the third mile, the trail became steeper and rockier. Aisha was far ahead now, sitting on a log and tapping her foot impatiently. When the others caught up, she frowned.

"Other teams are already passing us," she said. "Can't we go faster?"

"Grace's ankle is hurting," Jordan said. "We need to slow down."

Grace's face turned red. "I'm sorry," she whispered. "I don't want to ruin it for everyone."

Aisha opened her mouth to say something, then stopped. She looked at Grace's ankle, then looked away. For a moment, nobody spoke.

"Here," Tyler said suddenly, opening his backpack. He pulled out two lightweight trekking poles. "My dad made me bring these. I don't really need them. They take pressure off your legs."

Grace tried them. She smiled for the first time all day. "That's so much better."

They continued. Aisha walked a little slower now, staying close to the group. And as they climbed, something changed. They started talking. Aisha admitted that she always rushed because her older sisters were all star athletes, and she felt she had to be the best at everything. Grace said she was nervous that her ankle would never be strong again. Tyler said he often felt invisible at school because no one else cared about birds.

Then they looked at Jordan.

"What about you?" Aisha asked. "You've been really quiet."

Jordan hesitated. Then he took a deep breath. "I'm terrified of heights," he said. "Like, seriously terrified. I've been dreading this trip for weeks."

He expected them to laugh. But nobody did.

"Then why did you come?" Tyler asked.

Jordan shrugged. "I guess I didn't want to be afraid forever."

Aisha nodded slowly. "That's actually really brave."

At the fifth mile, they reached the hardest part of the trail: the Ridge. It was a narrow rocky path along the edge of the mountain, about as wide as a sidewalk. On one side was the solid mountain wall. On the other side was a long, steep slope covered in loose stones, falling away toward the forest far below. A metal safety rail ran along the edge, but Jordan's legs turned to jelly the moment he saw it.

He stopped. His heart pounded so hard he could hear it in his ears.

"I can't," he said. "I can't do it."

The others gathered around him. Grace leaned on her poles. "Hey," she said gently. "You helped me today when my ankle hurt. Now we'll help you."

Aisha stepped in front of him. "Don't look down. Look at my backpack. Just follow me, one step at a time."

Tyler moved behind him. "I'll be right here. And if you want, I'll tell you about every bird I see, so you have something else to think about."

Jordan almost laughed. Then he nodded.

They began to cross. Jordan kept his eyes fixed on Aisha's bright orange backpack. He kept one hand on the safety rail. Behind him, Tyler talked in a calm, steady voice.

"There's a mountain bluebird on that rock. They're bright blue, like a piece of the sky. And way up there, that's a golden eagle. Did you know golden eagles can see a rabbit from two miles away?"

Step by step, Jordan moved forward. His hands were sweaty. His legs were shaking. But he did not stop.

Finally, after what felt like forever, Aisha said, "You did it. Look."

Jordan looked up. They were across. The trail widened into a grassy slope, and just ahead was a wooden sign: "Eagle Peak Summit: 0.5 miles."

The last half mile was the happiest walk of Jordan's life. When they finally reached the summit, the whole world seemed to open up below them. Green forests stretched in every direction. Silver lakes sparkled in the valleys. Far away, snowy mountains touched the clouds. The wind was cool and fresh, and the sun warmed their faces.

They were the last team to arrive. But when Coach Harper saw them, he started clapping. Then the whole class joined in.

"I was watching from up here," Coach Harper said. "I saw how you helped each other on the Ridge. That's exactly what this challenge is about. Reaching the top together is much harder than reaching it first."

The four of them sat on a flat rock, sharing sandwiches and looking at the view. Tyler passed around his binoculars, and for the first time, Aisha was the one who asked him questions about birds. Grace stretched out her ankle in the sunshine and smiled.

Jordan looked down at the trail they had climbed, winding far below like a thin ribbon. He waited for the fear to come. But this time, it didn't.

"You know," he said, "it doesn't look so scary from up here."

Aisha bumped his shoulder with hers. "That's because you're not alone anymore."
