SKIN TYPE ADAPTATIONS AND TOLERANCE BUILDING

Adapting this routine to your specific skin type is essential for long-term success. For those with dry skin, the focus should be on layering hydrating toners or essences before the peptide serums. This provides a reservoir of moisture that helps the skin tolerate the potential drying effects of Retinol. For oily or combination skin, opt for oil-free or gel-based formulations of Copper Peptides and Matrixyl 3000 to ensure that the skin receives the necessary firming support without clogging pores or feeling heavy.

Tolerance building is particularly important for Retinol. Start by using the product two to three times per week, gradually increasing the frequency as your skin demonstrates resilience. THE BIOPHYSICAL ROLES OF L-ASCORBIC ACID, AZELAIC ACID, AND HYALURONIC ACID

To fully appreciate how these ingredients cooperate, we must first examine their individual biochemical profiles and how they interact with the cutaneous microenvironment.

L-Ascorbic Acid: The Gold Standard of Antioxidant Defense

L-ascorbic acid is the most biologically active and thoroughly researched form of Vitamin C. Within the skin, it serves as a potent water-soluble antioxidant that neutralizes reactive oxygen species (ROS) generated by ultraviolet radiation, infrared light, and environmental pollution. Beyond its protective capabilities, L-ascorbic acid is a critical co-factor for prolyl hydroxylase and lysyl hydroxylase, the enzymes responsible for stabilizing and cross-linking collagen fibers. Consequently, it provides essential collagen synthesis support, helping to maintain skin elasticity and minimize the appearance of fine lines and wrinkles.
